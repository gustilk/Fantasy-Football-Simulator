Keep a separate 2025 statistical baseline alongside 2026 observed history, including the DraftKings workflow. Preserve current rosters, roles, availability and frozen saved simulations; increase current-season influence as known observations accumulate.

**Why:** The user explicitly selected this approach. They want prior-season evidence without replacing current-season history or treating last year's lineup as the current lineup.

**How to apply:** Construct new forecast inputs from the separate sources. Never append prior-year games to current-season records, reactivate unavailable players from historical performance, or silently redefine frozen-model replay.

Overlapping fantasy, scrimmage, scoring and red-zone totals describe many of the same events as the basic passing/rushing/receiving lines. Importing a source does not justify another additive performance multiplier.

**Why:** The user's source list deliberately spans overlapping tables; repeated representations of the same touchdowns, yards and catches are not independent evidence.

**How to apply:** Identify directly modeled fields versus supporting references. Require a nonduplicative rationale and held-out evidence before adding further influences or claiming improved predictive accuracy.

Conservative identity and role matching should not blindly transfer last season's opportunity volume to a new team or promote a former backup's per-appearance volume into a current starter forecast.

**Why:** Historical workload depends on team, availability and role. Copying it without checking those contexts can contradict the requirement to keep current roles authoritative.

**How to apply:** Disclose excluded/missing matches. Any future relaxation should distinguish role-neutral efficiency from team-dependent workload and preserve unknowns rather than inventing zeros.
